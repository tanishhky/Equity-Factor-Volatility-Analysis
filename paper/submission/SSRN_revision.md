**Title:** Calm Months, Crash Months: Why Volatility Management Pays for Momentum and Not the Market

**Abstract:** Built with only past data, capped leverage and trading costs, volatility management improves momentum, from a Sharpe ratio of 0.43 to 0.94 over July 1973 to August 2026, but none of the other Fama-French factors. We explain why. Sorting stocks on past returns loads momentum on the market in proportion to the market's own return over the formation window. Using Tweedie's formula, we derive this beta in closed form from two cross-sectional quantities, the dispersion of stock betas and the density of past returns at the portfolio breakpoints, and test it on CRSP with no fitted parameter. Against realized betas from 1932 to 2025, the prediction has a slope of 0.92 (standard error 0.19) and 79% of the explanatory power of an in-sample regression: it captures how momentum's beta moves, while the realized level sits 0.20 below the prediction. Months after market declines are 29% of the sample and supply 71% of the covariance through which timing adds to momentum's return. If the market's premium rises with its variance, the same logic implies that the market should not be volatility-timed; in our sample it is not improved by it.

**Keywords:** volatility-managed portfolios, momentum, time-varying beta, Tweedie's formula, factor timing
